## Why a 300 Series owner wants a scan gauge

The LandCruiser 300 Series is a brilliant vehicle, but its factory
instrument cluster is deliberately minimal. There's no transmission
temperature gauge, no EGT readout, no boost gauge, and no clear indication of
when the diesel particulate filter (DPF) is doing a regeneration. For a
$140k touring rig that spends its life towing vans, crossing the Simpson, or
crawling up hills in low range, those are exactly the numbers you want to
see.

The 300's 3.3L twin-turbo V6 diesel (F33A-FTV) and 10-speed automatic are
strong, but heat is the enemy when you're working them hard. The two
failure modes owners worry about most are:

- **Transmission overheating** when towing heavy in hot conditions, and
- **DPF problems** from too much low-speed, short-trip running, where the
  filter never gets hot enough to regenerate cleanly.

A scan gauge surfaces both, plus a stack of other live data, so you can back
off *before* something cooks — not after the dash finally lights up.

## What the ScanGauge 3 actually is

The ScanGauge 3 (often written SG3 or ScanGauge III) is a compact OBD2
monitor, trip computer and code reader made by Linear Logic. It's the
third-generation model and the big step over the long-running ScanGauge II
is the **capacitive colour touchscreen** and **automatic X-Gauge
configuration**.

In plain terms, it's a small screen you plug into your car's diagnostic
port that turns the data already flowing on the vehicle's network into
clean, configurable gauges. It does four jobs in one device:

1. **Digital gauges** — live readouts of engine and driveline data.
2. **Trip computer** — fuel economy, fuel used, cost per trip and per km,
   litres per hour, distance, and more, across five separate trip memories.
3. **Scan tool** — reads and clears engine trouble codes, including pending
   codes, so you're not at the mercy of a single dash warning light.
4. **Alarms** — visual and audible warnings when a gauge crosses a threshold
   you set (e.g. trans temp over a chosen limit).

There's an Australian-delivered version sold through local retailers with
local warranty support, which matters for firmware and service.

## The gauges that matter on a 300 Series

You can run up to nine gauges per screen, but most 300 owners settle on a
core set. For a touring/towing 300 Series diesel, the high-value ones are:

- **Transmission fluid temperature** — the single most-requested gauge for
  the 300. Watch this closely when towing or in low range on a hot day.
- **Exhaust Gas Temperature (EGT)** — early warning of how hard the engine
  is working and a key signal of DPF and combustion health.
- **DPF status / soot load and regeneration** — finally lets you *see* when
  a regen is happening and how long it runs, so you can avoid switching the
  engine off mid-regen.
- **Boost / turbo pressure** — confirms the twin-turbo system is behaving
  under load.
- **Coolant temperature** — the real number, not just the factory "all good"
  needle.
- **Fuel rail pressure** — a useful diesel health indicator.
- **Instant + average fuel economy** — genuinely useful for trip planning
  and seeing the towing penalty in real time.

Being able to glance at trans temp and EGT while towing up a long grade is
the whole point — these are the readings that let you ease off or change
down *before* you generate heat you'll regret.

## X-Gauge: getting the Toyota-specific data

Standard OBD2 only exposes a limited, generic set of parameters. The
interesting 300 Series data — trans temp, EGT, DPF soot, turbo data — lives
in **manufacturer-specific** parameters that a generic reader can't see.

This is where **X-Gauge** comes in. X-Gauge lets the ScanGauge read those
extra Toyota-specific values. Historically (on the ScanGauge II) you had to
manually enter long hex command strings sourced from forums. The ScanGauge 3
adds **automatic X-Gauge configuration**: it detects the vehicle and offers
the relevant gauges, which dramatically reduces the fiddle for LC300 owners.

A couple of practical notes:

- The 300 Series is electronically complex (a lot of control modules), so
  there can be more than one candidate code for something like transmission
  temperature. The community on forums such as IH8MUD and the various 300
  Series Facebook groups regularly share confirmed, working setups — worth a
  search if a particular gauge needs tuning.
- Because the unit gets **Wi-Fi firmware updates**, vehicle support and
  auto-detection continue to improve after purchase.

## Installation on the 300 Series

This is genuinely a five-minute job:

1. Locate the OBD2 port — on the 300 Series it's under the dash on the
   driver's side, above the pedals.
2. Plug the ScanGauge 3 lead into the port. The unit is powered by the port,
   so there's no wiring, fuses or battery work.
3. Route the cable up to your chosen mounting spot. The unit can sit up to
   ~1.8 m from the port, so you've got room to place it on the dash, A-pillar,
   or near the cluster.
4. Let it auto-detect the vehicle, then add the gauges you want and arrange
   your screens.

No cutting, no splicing, and it's completely removable — handy if the
vehicle is under warranty or you move it between cars.

## Living with it: day-to-day, touring and towing

Where the ScanGauge 3 earns its keep on a 300:

- **Towing:** trans temp and EGT in view so you can manage heat on long
  climbs and in summer.
- **Remote touring:** the code reader means you can read *and clear* a fault
  in the field instead of limping to a dealer. The 300 has a lot of modules
  and can throw nuisance codes; being able to see what's actually going on
  is reassuring when you're a long way from help.
- **DPF management:** seeing regen activity helps you avoid short-tripping a
  vehicle into a DPF problem, and avoid shutting down mid-regen.
- **Fuel and trip data:** the trip computer is more detailed than the factory
  display and the five memories let you separate (say) a towing leg from
  daily driving.

The touchscreen makes day-to-day interaction far nicer than the old
button-driven gauges, and saved screens mean you can flick between a
"towing" layout and an "economy" layout.

## ScanGauge 3 vs the alternatives

- **vs ScanGauge II:** the SG3 adds the colour touchscreen, automatic
  X-Gauge configuration and Wi-Fi updates. The II still works and is
  cheaper, but the III is far less fiddly to set up on a 300.
- **vs a phone + Bluetooth OBD dongle (e.g. an app-based reader):** dongles
  are cheaper but rely on your phone, can be flaky on manufacturer-specific
  data, and tie up the screen you also use for navigation. The ScanGauge 3 is
  a dedicated, always-on, glanceable gauge — better for towing where you want
  the numbers permanently in view.
- **vs a custom analogue gauge install (e.g. plumbed-in trans temp sender):**
  a physical sender is arguably the "purest" reading but means cutting into
  lines and a real install. The ScanGauge reads the factory sensors with zero
  plumbing.

## FAQ

**Does the ScanGauge 3 show transmission temperature on a LandCruiser 300
Series?**
Yes — transmission fluid temperature is one of the main reasons 300 owners
fit one. It reads it via X-Gauge from the vehicle's own sensor. Some owners
fine-tune which code they use with help from the 300 Series community.

**Will it show DPF regeneration?**
Yes. You can monitor DPF status/soot load and see when a regen is running —
useful for avoiding short-trip DPF issues and not shutting down mid-regen.

**Do I need to wire it in?**
No. It plugs into the OBD2 port and is powered from it. No hard wiring, and
it's fully removable.

**Will it void my warranty?**
It's a non-invasive, removable plug-in device that reads (and clears) data —
it doesn't modify the vehicle. As always, check your own warranty terms, but
it's not a tune.

**Is it legal/safe to leave plugged in?**
Yes, it's designed to stay connected. Position it so it doesn't obstruct your
view or controls.

**ScanGauge 3 or ScanGauge II for a 300?**
For a 300 Series, the ScanGauge 3 is the easier choice thanks to automatic
X-Gauge setup and the touchscreen — less manual code entry to get trans temp
and EGT working.
